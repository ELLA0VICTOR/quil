# Quil — demo recording script

Allow roughly five to six minutes, including clicks and short pauses. The submission's maximum recording length has not been supplied; check it before recording. Read only the quoted paragraphs. The screen directions are for you.

## Before recording

- Use the same version you successfully tested, including the business view and send confirmations.
- Open four tabs: Quil's homepage, the second inbox you control, the earlier tested email conversation, and the matching research run in Mozaik Cloud. Have the accepted test invitation available too.
- Keep Suzan Hair Salon Manchester, or another approved live opportunity you actually tested, saved and easy to reopen.
- Stay signed in and connected. Show the connected-account panel rather than repeating Google's setup or authorization during the recording.
- Choose one live research run, one reviewed test email, and one incoming reply as the live demonstrations. Show the earlier price discussion and accepted invitation as completed-test evidence.
- Copy the brief and test messages below into a separate notes window. Keep credentials and unrelated inbox messages off screen.
- Move slowly enough to read the screen. If the recording rules permit editing, trim long loading waits while preserving the actual sequence. Introduce previous-test evidence explicitly.

## 1. The problem and introduction — homepage hero

**Screen:** Start at the top of the homepage. Move the pointer across the particle scene and let the cube/tree animation play. Begin scrolling near the end of this passage.

> If you're a freelancer or a small agency, finding your next client can take almost as much effort as doing the work. You search for businesses, check their websites, work out whether your service would help, and write a message that actually makes sense for them.
>
> Even then, the first search result can be misleading. A business might already have a website, or the opportunity you imagined might disappear when you look closer.
>
> Introducing Quil: a small AI sales crew that helps you move from finding a business to starting a useful conversation.

## 2. Meet the crew — slowly scroll the homepage

**Screen:** Scroll through “A warmer way to get started,” pause on the five mascots, then show “A first impression. A second opinion.” Briefly reach the footer. Use its “Try now” button to enter the app.

> Quil splits that work between five agents. Pip finds businesses. Milo investigates their websites and booking options. Iris checks the evidence. Wren prepares the offer and messages. Quil brings their work together into one answer.
>
> We built their collaboration with Mozaik. Think of it as their shared workspace: agents can work at the same time, exchange findings, and respond when something changes. For example, if a website turns up in another source, the team can change or withdraw the pitch before presenting it.

## 3. Give the crew a task — live research

**Screen:** Let the welcome screen appear for a moment. Enter the brief below. Keep the active mascots and their changing notes visible. After the narration, allow a short quiet pause for their work.

**Brief to paste:**

```text
I build booking websites. Find hairdressers in Manchester.
```

> In the app, I describe what I offer, who I want to reach, and where. Here, I'm looking for hairdressers in Manchester who might benefit from a booking website.
>
> The activity above the conversation follows the agents' actual progress. Milo investigates while Iris reviews independently. I can also pause, resume or cancel the search, and refresh without starting the same research again.

If this run produces no approved opportunity, show that result, then say: **“I'll use an approved result from my earlier live test for the outreach.”** Open the saved result; do not describe it as a result from the new run.

## 4. Check why the business was recommended

**Screen:** Open **See the evidence** on the selected live result. Show the source record and briefly open its public source if useful. Keep the business's actual recommendation visible.

> Here's a potential opportunity, with the records behind it. In this case, the evidence mentions manual bookings. That gives us a relevant question to ask: would a booking page help?
>
> Quil keeps that separate from claiming the business has no website or that its bookings are broken. I can inspect the sources myself, and review why other businesses were set aside when there are other checks in the result.

Use the manual-booking explanation only for a result that actually contains that evidence. If you use Suzan from Saved, introduce it as the earlier live result.

## 5. Shape the offer and save the work

**Screen:** In the business view, switch to **Offer**, briefly show **Introduction**, and make one small edit. Close it, save the business if necessary, then open **Saved**. Point to **Export saved businesses** without downloading unless needed.

> Wren prepares an introduction and a tailored offer that I can review and edit. The offer also includes suggested follow-ups and a meeting agenda, so I have a practical starting point for the conversation.
>
> I can save the opportunity to my account, return to its evidence and edited drafts, remove it later, or export my saved businesses. The sections stay together in one view, so I can switch between them and return to the results easily.

## 6. Show the Google connection

**Screen:** Close the business view and Saved. Click the envelope in the workspace header, show the connected Gmail/Calendar account, then close the panel and reopen the selected business's **Contact** view.

> Each signed-in user has their own workspace and can connect their own Gmail and Google Calendar. My account is already connected here.
>
> Quil prepares the work, and I approve the exact message or invitation before it goes out. For this demonstration, I'm using a second inbox that I control.

## 7. Send and receive — the short live communication test

**Screen:** Open **Contact → Write**. Select **Contact the business**. Replace the recipient with your second inbox, confirm it, and paste the subject and message below. Leave “Include the offer” unchecked for this short test. Select **Prepare approval preview**.

**Subject:**

```text
Quil demo — booking page
```

**Message:**

```text
Hi, I can offer one booking page and handover for USD 500, delivered in 10 working days after we confirm the requirements. Would that be useful?

This message is part of my Quil demo between two accounts I control.
```

> I'll send a short introduction now. The preview shows the sender, recipient and exact message. Once I've checked those, I approve the send through my connected Gmail.

**Screen:** Click **Approve & send message**. Let the success tick remain visible for a beat, then select **View conversation**. Switch to the second inbox, open the delivered email and reply in that same thread:

```text
Could you do it for USD 300?
```

**Screen:** Return to Quil and select **Read conversation** or **Refresh replies**. Show the received reply.

> The email has arrived in my second inbox. I'll reply there, then return to Quil and check the conversation. Here is the incoming reply, alongside my original message. I can continue working from the same place.

## 8. Show completed-test proof — pricing, follow-ups and meeting

**Screen:** Switch to the earlier tested Gmail thread. Show the USD 300 counteroffer and Quil's reviewed USD 400 reply. Briefly show the terms in Quil if they are still available. Then show the earlier invitation under **Contact → Activity**, its accepted status and, if useful, the matching Calendar event.

> We also tested what happens after that first reply. Here is the completed price discussion: the quote was five hundred dollars, the minimum was four hundred, and the reply asked for three hundred. Quil prepared a response at four hundred, using the scope and delivery terms I supplied, and I reviewed it before sending.
>
> Quil also prepares day-three and day-seven follow-ups. It checks for replies before sending, and those follow-ups still need my approval.
>
> Here is the meeting from the earlier test. Quil checked my calendar, prepared the invitation, and tracked the attendee's acceptance. That completes the handoff from research to a scheduled conversation.

Show only the actual earlier messages and event you tested. “Invitation sent” and “Invitation accepted” are different states; point to the accepted state when saying acceptance.

## 9. Show how the technology powers the work

**Screen:** Switch to the Mozaik Cloud research trace. Show the named agents, recorded steps and timing. Use a trace from the run you demonstrated, or explicitly say it is a previous test run. Return to Quil for the closing sentence.

> This is the crew's research trace in Mozaik Cloud. It makes the agents' work and timing visible. Mozaik Core runs their coordination, while Serper supplies search records and OpenAI helps interpret findings and prepare responses. Website checks add evidence from the public pages themselves.
>
> The interface is built with React and TypeScript, with Vercel hosting and Neon storing accounts, research and saved work. Caching and spending limits help keep repeated research affordable.
>
> Quil helps freelancers turn a possible lead into a researched opportunity, an editable offer, and a conversation they can follow through.
